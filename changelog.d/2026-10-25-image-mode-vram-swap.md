- **Image mode, step one: asking for a picture now sets the everyday chat aside for the draw, then brings it back (2026-10-25, V1 backend).** The picture-maker could not use this PC's 12 GB card while the everyday model held it, so a draw now takes TWO requests: the first answers `409` with `needs: "free_card"`, a plain sentence saying the chat will pause for about five minutes and come back, and the measured estimate; nothing is unloaded and nothing is drawn. The same request sent again with `"free_card": true` is the owner's consent, and the route unloads the everyday chat lane (`keep_alive: 0` - the lane's settings untouched), draws, asks ComfyUI to release its models (its own `POST /free`), and warms the everyday chat back, reporting both wall-clock times in the answer's `card` block and in words. The warm-back runs in a `finally`, so a refused or crashed draw never leaves the chat unloaded; only the PC may consent (a phone's confirmed swap is `403 pc_only`), and there is still no approval card - the card belongs to the "Making pictures" switch, which stays off by default. Way A (the recommended one) is the swap; way C never touches the chat; way B is recorded but not wired and says so. The wait was raised from the old 120-second cap to `DRAW_WAIT_S = 600` because a real Chroma1-HD draw on this PC was measured at 274.4 s. `docs/JARVIS-API.md` §129.4b records the contract, and `backend/test_pictures.py` proves the 409, the order (aside before draw, free and warm-back after), the error-path warm-back, way B's sentence, and the phone refusal with the card module stood in for. **Not proved by that suite:** that ComfyUI draws a real picture here and that the warm-back really reloads the model in a measured number of seconds - those need the real card and program, and are measured on the owner's PC.
