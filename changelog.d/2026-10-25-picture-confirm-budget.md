- **A slow picture draw no longer looks like a failure on either app (2026-10-25).** One drawing on the PC can legitimately take up to **1,680 s** in the worst case - the five ceilings are `unload_lane(timeout=120)` 120 s, `DRAW_WAIT_S = 600` 600 s, the `POST /free` call's own `FREE_WAIT_S` 180 s, the `/system_stats` poll's second `FREE_WAIT_S` 180 s and `warm_lane(timeout=600)` 600 s, and 120 + 600 + 180 + 180 + 600 = 1,680 s (`backend/jarvis_picture_card.py:526` and `:564-565`; `backend/jarvis_pictures.py:197` and `:208`) - but both apps gave the one long silent request only 1,200 s, so a legitimate slow draw plus the chat warm-back could outlast the app and the owner saw a false failure. The desktop's `PICTURE_TIMEOUT` and the phone's `CONFIRM_TIMEOUT_S` are now **1,740 s**, a minute over that sum, and the "about 20 minutes" wording where it described the client wait is now "about 29 minutes". `FREE_WAIT_S` is counted twice because `free_models()` calls `/free` with `timeout=FREE_WAIT_S` and *then* sets `deadline = time.time() + FREE_WAIT_S` for its `/system_stats` poll: the earlier draft of this change counted that step once, said four ceilings and 1,500 s, and was 180 s short. The relationship is pinned on all four sides so the two cannot drift apart again: a Rust unit test asserts the budget exceeds the 1,680 s sum and names each of the five terms, a JVM test asserts the same for the phone, the desktop source-pin suite reads the budget back out of `brain.rs`, and `backend/test_pictures.py` reads the PC's own defaults plus `free_models`' source (to prove the second wait is really there), asserts their sum is 1,680 s, and reads both clients' source to prove each budget is above it. No backend production code changed - the PC's own timeouts were correct and only the clients were too eager. The phone change is CI-verified only: there is no Android build, emulator or phone here.
