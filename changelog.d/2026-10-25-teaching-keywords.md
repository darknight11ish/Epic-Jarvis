- **A feature row must name words its lesson really uses (2026-10-25).** The teaching gate could not see the last lie in `features/features.json`: a row that names a lesson which never mentions the feature. "Save a draft to your Drafts folder" could name `home-calendar-email` - a lesson with no "draft" in it - and every rule stayed green, so "it is taught" was a claim nothing tested. New rule 7 in `tools/check_feature_list.py`: every taught row carries `must_mention`, 1-3 lowercase words or short phrases that must occur, whole word and case-insensitively, in the step `title`+`body` text of the lessons that row names. A missing word fails naming the feature, the lesson and the word; so does a taught row with no `must_mention` (the gate cannot be skipped) and a `must_mention` left on a row no lesson teaches. 104 of the 118 taught rows now carry 174 verified words, and the three copies of the file are kept byte-identical. The other 14 rows are a measured result rather than a silence: their lessons genuinely lack the subject - `home-calendar-email` has no "draft", "archive", "trash" or "tidy"; `when-something-is-wrong` no "rush", "latch" or "banner"; `limits-and-notes` no "update", "version" or "release" - so they are listed in `TEACHING_GAP` with the lesson and what it leaves out, a work list for the teaching side that empties itself (a row that gains keywords fails until its gap line is deleted). `backend/test_feature_list.py` proves the rule bites: a synthetic row whose made-up lesson lacks its word fails, naming all three, and the real list passes; all three defects planted in the real file at the desk failed the checker too. The checker reads step text only, never `where`, so a keyword cannot be earned by a breadcrumb. 139 features, 139 rows and 36 lessons are unchanged.
