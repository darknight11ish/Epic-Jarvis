- **The Galaxy gets its resolution back on a 30 Hz display, and the frame-budget test stops keeping the better of two samples (2026-10-28).** Two minor findings from the read-only audit of batch B1, fixed against `origin/main`. **Finding 4, the recovery.** `nextRenderScale` climbs only under `FAST_FRAME_MS` (19 ms), and a 30 Hz display hands out a frame every 33.3 ms even when the page does nothing at all, so once the picture had stepped down during a slow moment - a shader compiling, a window resize, another program taking the graphics card for a second - it stayed soft until the page was reloaded. Driven through the real rule, 40 frames at 33.3 ms left the scale at 0.25 (the floor) while 40 frames at 16.7 ms restored 1.0. Widening that threshold is the wrong fix, and this is the part worth keeping: 33.3 ms is also exactly what a machine that can only manage 30 frames a second produces, so a wider threshold would hand pixels to a machine that cannot afford them. The new `nextFrameScale` (pure, beside the old rule, which is unchanged) instead earns ONE probational step up - the same 0.75 factor as every other step - after three samples in a row that land within 3 ms of each other, in the band where the interval cannot tell a display from a slow machine. The answer is the very next sample: slower, and the picture goes back to the EXACT scale it was at (remembered, not multiplied back), the half-rate switch goes back with it, and the wait doubles - from 40 samples up to 2400 - before it asks again; not slower, and the pixels were free, so after another steady run it asks for the next step. That is why it cannot oscillate: a refusal is not a step down, so the picture never lands on a new operating point, and each refusal makes the next question rarer. Measured on the rule itself: a steady 33.3 ms display reaches full resolution in 18 samples (about four seconds at 30 Hz), a 33.0-33.6 ms one does the same, 45-60 ms medians never leave the floor and turn the half rate on exactly once, and a 30 Hz machine that is at its limit exactly is asked four times in 300 samples and ends where it started with its full frame rate. The half-rate switch is also latched now instead of re-derived from every sample, which is the other half of the same problem: while it is ON the intervals measure the display, not the work, so re-deriving it is what made it flap between thirty drawn frames and fifteen at precisely the machine class it exists for. Its threshold did not move, so one stall over `VERY_SLOW_FRAME_MS` still drops the picture to the floor and latches the half rate; what changed is that the latch is cleared by the first probe that succeeds instead of flapping every other sample. **Finding 8, the retest.** `tests/hud.mjs`'s G2 took one more sample after a failed first one and judged the BETTER of the two, so a real 16.7 ms regression that comes and goes could pass on one good sample. It now takes up to three samples once the first is over budget, judges the MEDIAN of what it measured, and prints every sample's p95 in the failure message and on the console, so a scheduling spike on a shared runner cannot fail a page that is fine while a regression that is really there cannot hide behind one good sample. The 16.7 ms bar is unchanged. **Files:** `galaxy-cosmos.js` and its two vendored copies are byte-identical (`tools/check_stale_twins.py` and `backend/test_hud_static.py` both pass, the latter 42 of 42), and `jarvis-desktop/tests/galaxy-cosmos.mjs` went from 42 to 47 checks with the five new scenarios. **Honest limits:** this is pure maths plus the browser suites - no 30 Hz display and no packaged WebView2 were driven here, so the feel on a real 30 Hz screen is what confirms it, and the frame-budget numbers in the audit's simulation are re-derived here as pure maths rather than re-measured on a GPU.
