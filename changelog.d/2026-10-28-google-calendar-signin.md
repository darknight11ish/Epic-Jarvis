- **Google Calendar, both ways: the private iCal link or a real Google sign-in, and only one at a time (2026-10-28):** the owner picked "Both at once" - a read-only private iCal link *and* a read/write Google sign-in - with the app choosing one. The sign-in is the Google "installed app" flow with a **loopback redirect** (`http://127.0.0.1:<random port>/callback`, bound to 127.0.0.1 only - no ngrok, no Cloudflare Tunnel, no Tailscale Funnel), **PKCE (S256)** and a random `state` that is checked on the callback: a wrong or missing state is refused and nothing is exchanged. The scope is `calendar.events` alone (the narrowest that can list, create, change and delete). The refresh token lives in Windows Credential Manager through the existing `jarvis_token_store` helper, access tokens live in memory only, and neither is ever written to a file, a log line, a route answer, a card or a model prompt. Reads raise no card; creating, changing or deleting an event raises ONE approval card showing the exact event, sends nothing before the yes, and is refused outright when the last read of the calendar is stale. `backend/jarvis_google_calendar.py` (+ its `jarvis-backend/` twin) with routes from its own `install()`, called by `plugins/ready/google-calendar` - no patch, no edit to `jarvis_hud.html`. New docs: `docs/GOOGLE-CALENDAR-SETUP.md` (the one-time Google Cloud setup, click by click, including the honest note that a "Testing" app's token expires after about 7 days and that publishing it needs Google's verification review) and JARVIS-API §161. Not built here, said plainly: the Settings row, the desktop chat tool and the phone - all three need doors in files the parallel account-connect work owns (`src-tauri/src/lib.rs`, `jarvis_agent.py`, `jarvis_quick.py`).
