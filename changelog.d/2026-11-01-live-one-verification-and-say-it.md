- **Jarvis Live: verify your voice once per session, and say the answer instead of pointing at the screen (2026-11-01, the owner's two reports).** (1) "Once I actively verify my voice in a single Jarvis Live session, Jarvis shouldn't need to re-verify my voice." It did: after ONE successful full-sentence check, `jarvis_live` still counted a **120-second window or 16 short clips**, and when that ran out the next short clip was answered with "Say a full sentence so I can re-check your voice." - the owner verifying again, in the same session, two minutes after he had already done it. The window is gone: one successful check verifies the whole session, so nothing asks again for as long as it lasts. `[live] trust_window_s` and `[live] trust_max_short_clips` are no longer read (an installed file still carrying them changes nothing), and the status payload keeps both keys with `window_s` = the session length and `max_short_clips` = 0. The strict check itself still runs **silently** on a >= 2.0 s clip, and that is on purpose: it is what refuses a voice that is not the owner's, and its verdict is what a private answer needs before it may be read aloud - the owner hears nothing about it. A failed or erroring check, a re-enrolled voice print, a new session or the other device all still leave the session unverified. (2) "It will say 'it's on your screen' when it'd clearly capable of reading the info out loud." The cause was not only wording: a 1.5-2.0 s clip running on the session's verification carried an empty `strictness`, so `_really_checked` was false and the reply said `memory_aloud: false` even though the session's own check had just proved the voice - so an answer that used what Jarvis remembers (read aloud by default, the owner's choice of 2026-09-24) was kept on screen and the app said "It's on your screen." The strictness the session's real check earned now travels with those clips, so the flags answer exactly as they did for the clip that passed; every setting that withholds an answer still withholds it (a sensitive saved fact, a private answer, the screen rule), and the apps' own read-aloud rules were not touched. The one Live instruction to the model (`jarvis_live.MODEL_NOTE`) also now says it: when it already has the answer in hand - a tool result, a page it read, a quick fact - it says it, and a screen line is for what it truly cannot say, such as a table of numbers, a picture or a long list. Tests: `backend/test_live.py` gained `t_one_verification_covers_the_whole_session` (one check, then a second turn with no speaker model and no ask; twenty short clips and fifteen minutes later still trusted; a new session asks again; a failed check, a voice-check error and a re-enrolled print each void it) and `t_session_verification_keeps_the_read_aloud_flags` (a memory answer is read aloud on the very next turn; a private answer and a sensitive fact still follow their own settings; a screen answer still stays on screen), and both were shown to fail without the change. The shared contract table was regenerated with `tools/gen_live_cases.py` (both app copies; `trust_window_s` 120 -> 7200, the two new honest keys) and the whole live fleet is green (1676 passed, 1 skipped, 0 failed). No new route, no new setting, no new disk file: the verification lives in memory for the life of the session.
