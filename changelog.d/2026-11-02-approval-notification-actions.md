- **An approval card can be answered from its Android notification, with Approve, Deny and Review in Jarvis (2026-11-02).** The owner asked: *"make sure i can approve jarvis cards through an android notification with a deny or approve button or tap too see more button if the summary that's short enough to fit into an android notification isn't long enough."* Notification cards already existed with Deny; this adds Approve under Gemini's binding five-point envelope. Deny still works locked, because refusing is safe. Every approve action is built with `setAuthenticationRequired(true)`, so Android itself will not deliver it until the device is unlocked. Only a small read-only card (the owner's 2026-11-01 exemption) may send its decision straight from the tap: it goes through EventService, which waits for a live link, re-reads the card, and re-checks that it is still small and still private before using the same `JarvisRuntime.decide` the app uses. Every write, config, send-file or phone-app card's Approve opens Jarvis on that card and runs the same `BiometricPrompt` the card's own button runs - forced even when the owner's setting alone would not have asked on the card. A card whose notification text is over 240 characters replaces Approve with Review in Jarvis, so a clipped filename or command is never approved sight-unseen; PC-only cards and multi-plan proposals get Review for the same reason. The lock screen gets `VISIBILITY_PRIVATE` with the public version **"Jarvis: 1 approval waiting"** and no card words. A redacted notification - App lock on (the default), hidden memory lists, or a phone with no secure lock - offers **Review in Jarvis and Deny, and no Approve**, because approving words the notification did not show is the blind approval the 240-character rule also forbids; turning App lock off is the owner's existing Security choice, not something this feature changes. A card that leaves `/api/pending` has its notification cancelled by the existing sync, now with its rule in the tested policy. No backend change, no new route, no second permission path, and `notice.approve_ok` is still ignored. Desk check: `jarvis-client` JVM suite **3,218 tests, 0 failed, BUILD SUCCESSFUL**, with 19 of them new in `ApprovalNotificationPolicyTest`; the one source pin this change altered was updated to the new envelope. NOT-TESTABLE without the phone: the shade drawing two buttons, the lock-screen refusal, the real prompt, and a direct approve reaching the PC after a process kill.
