- **The phone's "One moment." clip can no longer be played in the voice the owner replaced (2026-11-02).** The owner's report: *"in jarvis live on my android phone, the 'one moment' will be in the default male voice while the rest of the response from jarvis is in the female voice i choice"*. The cause, from the shipped code: the Live turn path (`VoiceSession.deliverLiveClip` - the only path a Live question takes, called from `LiveService.kt`) never re-read `flow` or the clip; only push-to-talk and the wake word did, in `deliver()` - so Live kept whatever clip a screen's last status read had fetched, however long ago; `toolStarted()` then played those bytes without comparing their key with `flow.moment.key`; and `refreshMoment()` kept the old bytes on any failed, empty or 503 fetch, so even a refresh could leave the old voice playable under the new key. The PC cannot hand out the old voice for a new key: `jarvis_voice_flow.moment()` only returns a clip whose key matches `moment_key()` (a hash of speaking voice, speed, face voice and engine) at the moment it is asked, which is why the answers themselves were female. The fix is phone-only: a `MomentClip` holder that hands out bytes only for the key in use now, clears them before asking for a new key's clip (so a 503 is silence, not yesterday's voice), re-reads `flow` at the start of every spoken turn including Live's, and retries one clip fetch once (700 ms) when a turn begins before the PC has made the clip. No new route, no new permission, no speech-to-text; the phone's "Say 'One moment' if I'm kept waiting" switch and the once-per-question rule are unchanged. Tests: 7 new JVM checks in `MomentClipTest` (old-key bytes never play, a new key means a fetch, a 503 leaves silence, overtaken bytes are dropped, once per question, `ready:false` is still fetched at turn start, and the shipped wiring), and the whole phone suite is green (3,117 tests, 0 failures, 0 errors, 0 skipped). Honest limits: no phone audio was heard from here, and the phone keeps no log of when its in-memory clip was fetched, so the exact fetch that supplied the male bytes is inferred from the code, not proved.
