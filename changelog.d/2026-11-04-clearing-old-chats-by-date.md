- **Clearing old chats by date, with a card, two yeses and a fingerprint: the backend half (2026-11-04).** The owner asked "how long does it take for you to clear old chats if i batched a lot of them together?" and Jarvis answered that it had no built-in "clear old chats" action and did not track how long it took - both false. `jarvis_history_purge.py` (shipped whole, `history-purge.patch` is its one install block) answers `POST /api/history/purge/preview`, `/confirm` and `/cancel`: a preview card listing every chat day by day, every fact by its own words, what is kept and why, and that it cannot be undone; then the FIRST yes, then a SECOND yes that needs a Windows Hello ticket (desktop, key in `~/.openjarvis/purge-auth.key`) or a fingerprint ticket (phone, key = the existing pairing token), valid 90 seconds, single use. **Pinned chats are kept unless the preview asked for them** - including a chat pinned after the card, which is skipped and counted. The facts it clears are only the facts whose recorded `meta.conversation_id` is one of the deleted chats, and their words are ERASED (`MemoryStore.erase`), not merely forgotten. Every deletion appends one line to `~/.openjarvis/purge-audit.jsonl` with counts, ids, the window, the auth method and the elapsed time - no title, no message, no fact's words. Measured on this PC: 120 chats / 720 messages deleted in **47 ms**, 500 / 3,000 in **94 ms**, 1,000 / 6,000 in **141 ms**, 2,000 / 12,000 in **265 ms** - batching does not make it slow. The same measurement is what Jarvis now answers with, from its own measured file and switches, instead of "I don't track". Honest limits: the backend checks a signed ticket, not a human - it cannot see the Windows Hello prompt or the fingerprint reader; and the bytes it reports as freed are an estimate.
