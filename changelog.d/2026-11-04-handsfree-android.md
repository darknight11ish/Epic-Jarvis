- **Hands-free mode on the phone: every approval card is read out, "confirm" or "deny" answers it by voice, and Jarvis can write a text message for the owner (2026-11-04).** A new Settings row (last on the screen, so it moves no other row), and the same switch from a typed chat sentence or said in Jarvis Live - one parser for both, answered by the phone rather than by the model. With it on, a card the PC raises arrives as a `handsfree_card` event: the phone fetches the PC's own WAV from `/api/voice/say` and plays it through its speaker (never during a phone call, and never in the phone's own voice if the PC has no voice engine - the card is then shown only, and no microphone opens), posts an ongoing notification naming the card and the time it has left with its own "Open the card" action, and then opens a ten-second answer window marked by the same two-note chime the talk button uses. The window records a 16 kHz mono WAV (`handsfree/HandsfreeClip.kt`) and uploads it to `POST /api/handsfree/answer`; **the phone never transcribes anything** - the PC transcribes it, checks the owner's voice print and decides, and the phone shows what it heard and decided in plain words ("Heard: \"yes, do it\" - approved.", "That was not your voice - nothing was approved.", "I didn't catch that - say confirm or deny."). `POST /api/handsfree/enabled` keeps the contract's asymmetry: ON may answer 202 with a card waiting and is never reported as on, OFF is immediate. `phone_command kind=send_sms` is carried out on the phone - the name is resolved in its own address book by an exact, case-folded, unique match (two numbers for one name sends nothing and says why; the same number twice is one match; a number the owner said is used as it is), `SEND_SMS` and `READ_CONTACTS` are runtime permissions asked for when the switch goes on, and `/api/phone/result` gets an answer for every command, with Android's own `RESULT_OK` as the only thing that ever means "sent". The microphone is arbitrated through one new ledger (`voice/MicOwner.kt`): the answer window holds it, and Jarvis Live and "hey Jarvis" close their recorders for those ten seconds and reopen afterwards, while the talk button is greyed with "Listening for your confirm/deny" under it. A PC without the feature answers 404 on all four routes and sends neither event, and every surface then says "Hands-free is not available on this PC yet." - no invented mode, no microphone. `jarvis-client`'s JVM suite is green at 3,366 tests (five new files: `HandsfreeFlowTest`, `HandsfreeClipTest`, `HandsfreeEventTest`, `MicOwnerTest`, `SmsSendTest`); four existing tests gained justified pins (the services-with-a-foregroundServiceType count 5 → 6, the wake word's pause condition, the jump list's last entry, and the two lists of Settings rows with no catalogue menu id). NOT TESTED, said plainly: no real call, voice clip or text message was driven from this branch - nothing was installed on the owner's Pixel 9, no SMS was sent, and the PC half of the contract does not exist yet, so the phone was built and proven against the frozen contract and a fake backend only.
