- **A screenshot attached in chat can be saved to your PC with one card (2026-11-23).** The owner attached a screenshot and typed *"transfer these to my PC"*; the answer was model prose - *"I can't transfer this screenshot to your PC myself"* - because no path existed: the chat picture path decodes a picture for the vision lane and writes nothing, and the upload path lands files only for bytes a phone sent in chunks. Now `"transfer this screenshot to my PC"` (and *"save/send/put this picture on my PC"*, *"transfer these to my PC"*) is answered deterministically: `jarvis_chat_picture.raw_pictures()` reads the picture's own bytes out of the request, `jarvis_upload.save_chat_picture()` stages exactly one file and raises the **same** `receive_uploaded_document` card the phone's folder door raises, and the upload module's own `finish` measures what arrived, checks the picture's header and does ONE `os.replace` into `From my phone` once a person says yes. A denied or timed-out card removes the staging and leaves nothing behind; the chat answers before the person decides, because a chat turn must not hang for the card's 15 minutes. The name comes from the picture's OWN header (`.png`, `.jpg`, `.webp`, `.gif`, `.bmp`), never from the phone, so `finish`'s header check cannot refuse the file it just wrote. A turn with no picture is refused in plain words, two pictures are refused as two cards, and *"send this to my PC"* (no picture named) stays the model's. `backend/test_chat_picture_save.py` (25 checks) proves the card-before-bytes rule, the byte-identical landing, the denial path, the refusals and the three sentences; `backend/test_upload.py` stays 212/0.
