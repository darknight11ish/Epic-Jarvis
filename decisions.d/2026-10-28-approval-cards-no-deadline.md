- **2026-10-28 — An approval card shows no time limit; a request whose wait ended is swept:** the owner ruled today, about approval cards: **"if there doesn't need to be a time limit don't have one."** This supersedes the half of the 2026-10-23 ruling (Q1) that kept every routine card on a visible 15-minute countdown. Q1's own record says why a limit existed for the cards it refused to exempt - **"a stale 'yes' on a card that loosens a rule is a time bomb"** - and that record also says its original wording "is not recoverable from the repository", so both halves are quoted here rather than paraphrased. **A first version of this change went too far and review caught it** (Gemini's F3, ruling f): removing #965's read-time sweep left a timed-out request's card in `/api/pending` with a live Approve that was guaranteed to fail with `409 LATE_APPROVAL` after Windows Hello - a "zombie Approve button" that regressed the "4 WAITING ON YOU" cards #965 had just removed. **The shipped synthesis:** (1) no screen draws a countdown and `pending()` sends no `expires_in` for any card - while a request is genuinely waiting, its card shows Approve and Deny and no clock; (2) **#965's read-time sweep is kept**, re-keyed on the moment the request stops waiting, which is written on the row as `waited_until` at the INSERT: once that plus the same 60-second grace has passed, `pending()` marks the routine row `expired` before it builds the list, so no client is handed a card whose Approve can only answer 409. It is a lapse, never a decision, and it is recomputed on the PC at the read; (3) the four ceremonies of the 2026-10-23 ruling (`pair_device`, `unretire_shared_key`, `register_approval_key`, `restore_backup`) carry `waited_until = NULL`, are never swept, and their request waits until its own flow withdraws the card, so they stay answerable as long as the owner needs; (4) the late-approve 409 stays as a race guard - inside the grace a stale page can still tap, and `_wait_open()` refuses it, `still_waiting()` answers the same question read-only, `decide()` refuses the APPROVE, and `jarvis_owner_check.approve_check()` answers 409 with one plain sentence ("That card is not waiting for you any more: the request that asked has finished, so nothing can be approved from it. Nothing was changed - ask Jarvis again if you still want it.") and withdraws the card at that moment, so it leaves rather than lingering. **A DENY always works**, including on a card whose request is gone: refusing is the safe direction and is how the owner clears an old card. **Why a literal "no time limit" was refused:** the thread that waits for an answer is the thread that runs the action. Holding it for ever is a resource leak on a `ThreadingHTTPServer`, it makes "Stop everything" unable to interrupt a wait, and a dozen modules (`jarvis_vault`, `jarvis_voice_enroll`, `jarvis_voices`, `jarvis_speech`) compute their OWN waits from `approval_timeout_seconds` and would give up while the card lived - the exact "approved, nothing ran" pair this project refuses to produce. **Nothing else is relaxed:** no card is ever auto-approved, no control approves more than one card, no client clears anything, nothing runs without a person, the Windows Hello / signed-approval receipt is unchanged, and there is no new public route. **Costs, said plainly:** inside the 60-second grace a card can still be on a screen whose queue was read just before `waited_until` passed, and a tap then answers 409 with the sentence (the race guard, not the routine path); a late Approve is refused rather than honoured, because nothing is left to run it; and a backend restart does not preserve a card whose request died with it. **Reality checked, not assumed:** the connect-account and credential hand-off cards are NOT gate cards - they are host-side in-memory cards with their own short TTL (`jarvis_connect_account.CARD_TTL_S`, `jarvis_secret_handoff`), they never enter `/api/pending`, and no request thread parks on them, so `APPROVAL_NEVER_EXPIRES` cannot and does not cover them; they keep their own TTL and are untouched here. Built in `backend/no-deadline-cards.patch` (applied after `no-expiry-cards.patch`), the shipped `backend/jarvis_owner_check.py` (+ its `jarvis-backend/` twin), and client copy on the desktop bar/widget/HUD and the phone; proven by `backend/test_gate_no_deadline.py` (70 checks: a routine card still pending inside the grace with no `expires_in`, the read sweep taking it after `waited_until + 60` so no live Approve is left, a late approval refused with those words and cleared, a deny still working, the four ceremonies answerable a day later and never swept, a withdrawn card gone, dead-process rows settled at start-up and on the next read, and no duplicate-request swallowing), with `test_pending_expiry_on_read.py` kept as the evidence for #965's sweep in the patch that introduced it.
