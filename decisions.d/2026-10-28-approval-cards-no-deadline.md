- **2026-10-28 — An approval card has no deadline; the request that asked does:** the owner ruled today, about approval cards: **"if there doesn't need to be a time limit don't have one."** This supersedes the half of the 2026-10-23 ruling (Q1) that kept every routine card on the 15-minute window. Q1's own record says why a limit existed for the cards it refused to exempt - **"a stale 'yes' on a card that loosens a rule is a time bomb"** - and that record also says its original wording "is not recoverable from the repository", so both halves are quoted here rather than paraphrased. Q1's narrowing (the four ceremonies whose cards waited without a deadline: `pair_device`, `unretire_shared_key`, `register_approval_key`, `restore_backup`) is not undone; it is no longer a special case, because no card has a clock now. **What changed:** a card is never taken away by time. `pending()` sends no `expires_in` for any card (never 0, never a large number), `check()` no longer marks the row `expired` when its wait ends, and the every-read sweep the 2026-10-28 "expired cards" fix added is removed from `pending()` - the card stays on every screen, with its buttons and no countdown, until the owner answers it or the PC withdraws the work. **What also had to change, and why a literal "no time limit" was refused:** the thread that waits for an answer is the thread that runs the action. Holding it for ever is a resource leak on a `ThreadingHTTPServer`, it makes "Stop everything" unable to interrupt a wait, and a dozen modules (`jarvis_vault`, `jarvis_voice_enroll`, `jarvis_voices`, `jarvis_speech`) compute their OWN waits from `approval_timeout_seconds` and would give up while the card lived - the exact "approved, nothing ran" pair this project refuses to produce. So the REQUEST keeps the shipped patience and records it on the row (`waited_until`); when it ends, the card stays and the ability to act on a "yes" ends with it. **Decision-time revalidation, which is what makes the late card honest:** `_wait_open()` reads the row's own `waited_until` against the PC's clock; `decide()` refuses an APPROVE for a row no request can still act on; `still_waiting()` answers the same question read-only; and `jarvis_owner_check.approve_check()` - the PC's own wrapper on `POST /api/approve` - refuses with ONE plain sentence ("That card is not waiting for you any more: the request that asked has finished, so nothing can be approved from it. Nothing was changed - ask Jarvis again if you still want it.") and withdraws the card at that moment. It is a lapse, never a denial. **A DENY always works**, including on a card whose request is gone: refusing is the safe direction and is how the owner clears an old card. **Nothing else is relaxed:** no card is ever auto-approved, no control approves more than one card, no client clears anything, nothing runs without a person, the Windows Hello / signed-approval receipt is unchanged, and there is no new public route. **Start-up** still settles rows no living request can act on (a lapsed `waited_until`, a pre-patch row with none), and still leaves the four ceremonies alone; the 60-second grace is kept as a bookkeeping margin only, because `_wait_open()` refuses an approval at the exact moment, so the grace can never turn a late tap into an approved-with-nothing-done. **The costs, said plainly:** a late Approve is refused rather than honoured (nothing can run it); and a backend restart does not preserve a card whose request died with it - the card was never going to be answerable to anything. Built in `backend/no-deadline-cards.patch` (applied after `no-expiry-cards.patch`), the shipped `backend/jarvis_owner_check.py` (+ its `jarvis-backend/` twin), and client copy on the desktop bar/widget/HUD and the phone; proven by `backend/test_gate_no_deadline.py` (63 checks: a routine card still pending past the old fifteen minutes, a late approval refused with those words and cleared, a deny still working, the four ceremonies unchanged, a withdrawn card gone, start-up settling, and no duplicate-request swallowing), with the earlier suites kept as evidence for the patches they are about.
