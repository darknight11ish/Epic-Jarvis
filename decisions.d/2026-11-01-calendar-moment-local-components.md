- **2026-11-01 — A calendar moment is built from its local components, never from midnight plus absolute minutes:** The desktop HUD's calendar computed an event's moment as local midnight plus the clock time in absolute milliseconds, which is right on ordinary days and wrong by an hour on both daylight-saving change days (a 23- or 25-hour local day); on 2026-11-01 an event at 12:14 read "past" five minutes after it began. Decided: a moment is always asked from the PC as its own local date and clock time (`new Date(year, month, day, hours, minutes)`), so the time the owner reads is the time the event happens, every day; the same shape the PC's own calendar store already uses, and the phone never had the bug because it keeps local date strings and no instant arithmetic. The rule is pinned by five fixed-date cases in `jarvis-desktop/tests/hud-calendar.mjs` (both transition directions, the all-day arm and the midnight rollover) - fixed dates, because CI runs in UTC where the old arithmetic could never fail, so the value is that the bug can no longer hide on a Pacific PC. Stated limit: proved by the page's own helpers under Node, not yet by watching the installed window cross a change day.
