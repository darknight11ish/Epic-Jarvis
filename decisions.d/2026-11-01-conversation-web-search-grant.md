- **2026-11-01 — A conversation-scoped web-search approval, granted with ONE card, is allowed; nothing else widens:** The owner asked, in their own words, *"if I ask jarvis and say you have full approval to search the web for anything during this conversation. give jarvis the approval to search the web for the entire conversation until I say it doesn't have authorization to do that. make sure a card comes up that I have to approve before this happens"*, and then *"and if I ask anything further like book a flight for me online then it will have to have another card to approve further action beyond just searching the web."* Decided: a standing permission is allowed only in this one shape - ONE action (`search_the_web`), ONE conversation (keyed on the request's own `conversation_id`), granted only by a person answering a card, revocable instantly with no card, capped at 8 hours, held in memory only so a restart ends it, outranked by Lockdown, and logged on every use with the grant's id. The alternative - letting the grant follow the words ("full approval to do anything") - was rejected because a model's reading of an open-ended sentence is not a permission: the grant's scope is fixed by the ACTION, and every other action keeps its own card. The other alternative, a setting that survives restarts, was rejected for the same reason: "until I say it doesn't have authorization" is a promise about a conversation, not a preference to keep. Refusing to grant at all when the request carries no conversation id is deliberate: a grant that cannot be limited to one chat is not what was asked for. Two new model tools (`approve_web_search_for_conversation`, `stop_web_search_approval`), two new gate actions (`grant_web_search_conversation` at tier `ask`, `revoke_web_search_grant` at tier `auto` - a card to STOP what the owner just asked to stop would be wrong), one new patch (`backend/web-search-grant.patch`, three additive hunks in `jarvis_gate.py`), and the store, the tools and the one place a grant is read in the shipped `jarvis_agent.py`. Proved by `backend/test_web_search_grant.py` (95 checks across all nine cases, driven through the real turn loop, the real `jarvis_search` planning and the real `jarvis_gate` audit writers, with the patch rehearsed forwards and backwards byte for byte), with `tools/check_patch_original_text.py` pinning the three hunks and 14 lines of the owner's own text that the patch has to read.
