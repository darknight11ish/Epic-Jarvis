- **2026-11-02 — How long Jarvis Live waits before it turns off is the owner's setting, changeable by asking and in Settings:** The owner asked: *"also id likee to he able to tell jarvis to change the setting that turns off jarvos live by extending how long ot takestoo turn off when it doesntt hear anything"*. Decided: the silent window that ends an idle Live session stops being `jarvis_live.py`'s constant `QUIET_S = 90` (whose own comment said the owner's call was pending) and becomes `[live].quiet_s` in the owner's settings file, with ONE row owning it - `jarvis_limits.LIMITS`' `live_idle_seconds` - and one reader, `jarvis_live.quiet_seconds()`, used by the engine's tick and by the status both apps draw. The default is 90 seconds, exactly today's behaviour, so nothing changes on a PC that never touches it. The range is 30 seconds to 10 minutes, and it is bounded for two real reasons: below 30 seconds Live would end in the pause between two thoughts (the voice check alone needs a 2-second clip), and above 10 minutes an open microphone has been forgotten, which is the very risk that window exists to bound; the engine clamps a hand-edited value to the range as well as the route. It is changeable BOTH by asking Jarvis (chat or Live: "make Jarvis Live wait three minutes" changes it and is answered with the new value and the range in plain words; a length outside the range is refused in words that name the range and nothing is written) AND in Settings on the desktop ("Limits and frequency") and the phone (Limits), which draw the same row and therefore the same label, words and range - the spoken sentence goes through `jarvis_settings_registry.set_live_idle` -> `jarvis_limits.change`, the exact function `POST /api/limits/settings` calls, so it adds no writer and no range check of its own. No approval card in either direction: Live must already be running (the owner started it, the microphone was already open), the number only decides when an idle session ends, it never starts Live, reads, sends or stores anything, and the same session's time limit already extends instantly by saying "give me twenty more minutes" - asking a card to keep listening longer would be stricter than the control beside it. `GET /api/limits` gained one additive field, `step` (how far one tap of the -/+ moves a row; 30 on this row so three minutes is three taps from the default, 1 elsewhere), which both apps read and an older screen may ignore.
