- **2026-11-03 — A scheduled task may run any job in the owner's own words, and one that would ask its own card is refused rather than scheduled as two:** The owner's decision of 2026-11-02 was that a scheduled task may record the job in his own words, and that at fire time Jarvis starts it and raises ONE card naming exactly what it will do, with no shell, command or script kind. This slice builds that: a reminder's `run-task` action may carry `job` (the owner's phrase, at most 240 characters) instead of only the closed `briefing`/`backup` names, resolved through the same `jarvis_quick.match` parse half chat uses when the reminder is saved and again when it fires, and run through `jarvis_quick.run` - the same chat path, so there is no executor, no command line and no second interpreter. The batch audit of 2026-11-03 found the one place the first cut broke the promise: a phrase resolving to an intent that raises its OWN approval card (`forget_range`, `update_start`, a setting change that asks, a repeating briefing, "read this link", and the rest read off `jarvis_quick.run`'s dispatch) produced a second card after the scheduler's `schedule_action` card. The PR had disclosed that as a deviation; the owner's promise is kept instead. Such a phrase is refused when the reminder is saved, in one plain sentence that says it needs the owner's approval each time, that it therefore cannot be scheduled as one card, and which action the words matched - nothing is silently dropped, no other intent is substituted, and no reminder is written. A stored row that nevertheless resolves to a carded intent at fire time (an older build, or an upgrade that changed the grammar) is refused there too: no second card, nothing runs, the audit says `needs_approval`, and the row's own view says it did not run and why. Every intent `jarvis_quick.py` can construct is classified carded or cardless, with a test that fails when a new one arrives unclassified; an unknown intent is refused conservatively in its own sentence rather than assumed cardless. Both apps' Reminders screens still send only the closed `task` name, and that one-sided gap is recorded in `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §8 as a deliberate follow-up rather than built here.
