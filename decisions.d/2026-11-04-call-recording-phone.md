- **2026-11-04 — Call recording is armed by the owner's own keyword on the phone, and the phone records its MICROPHONE because Android blocks the call's own stream:** The owner asked for Jarvis to record a call when he says "record call", with the PC transcribing it, dating it, identifying the speakers and storing it as a new Obsidian note named with the date and time. Three parts of that decide the shape and none of them is a taste: (1) the call rings on the phone and Android does not let a normal app capture the call's own audio - `VOICE_CALL` is privileged, and a sideloaded app never holds it - so the recording is the phone's microphone with the call on **speaker**, `UNPROCESSED` where the platform opens it and `MIC` where it does not, and **never `VOICE_COMMUNICATION`**, whose echo canceller removes exactly the far end the feature exists to keep; the service asks Android to put the call on speaker and, when it refuses, the notification says "Turn the call on speaker so Jarvis can hear both sides" rather than claiming both sides were captured. (2) The phone never turns speech into words and never writes to the vault: it posts PCM16LE mono 16 kHz to the owner's own PC in 64 KiB pieces, with the date the call began and a `consent_notice: "on"` flag, and the PC transcribes, dates, works out who was speaking and names the note. (3) The recording is safe before it is sent: every piece is written to this app's own files directory first and uploaded after, a failed chunk is retried with the SAME sequence number - and a 409 that names the number the PC wants (`expected`) means the pieces below it are already there, so the phone records those as sent and sends the real next one rather than retrying the same number for ever (the 2026-11-05 privacy audit's D3) - and a recording the PC has not taken is kept and offered again, with its own notification saying so, and can be deleted from the phone by the owner behind a confirmation that says it cannot be undone (D7). A persistent notification runs for the whole call with the elapsed clock, Stop, and the one-line reminder "Recording - tell the other person", and it is redacted on a locked phone by the same rule every other Jarvis notice uses. The microphone is arbitrated by ONE point (`voice/MicOwner.kt`, held in `MicNow`): while call recording is armed or recording, Jarvis Live, "hey Jarvis" and the talk button **pause** their recorders and resume when it is released - paused rather than stopped because Android 14+ refuses to start a microphone foreground service from the background, so a stopped Live could not come back until the app was in front, and "paused and restored" would quietly become "off until the owner notices". The armed state is visible in one line ("Call recording armed" / "Recording call 00:04:12") in Settings, Voice, which is also the one place to arm, stop and re-send. A recording under a second is the one thing deleted, and it says so; a low battery, an unplugged headset, a refused microphone and the app shutting down all end the recording the same way - close the microphone first, then finish the upload. The routes are `POST /api/call/recording/start`, `/chunk` and `/finish` (the frozen contract of 2026-11-04), classified `phone-only` in `tools/check_parity.py`, and `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` section 8 carries the one-sided reasoning: the PC has no microphone in that call and must not, the phone has no transcriber and cannot see the vault, so each half does the only half it can. **A real call was NOT placed from this session** - the owner's own Pixel 9 is the one real-call field test, after this PR and the PC half land.
