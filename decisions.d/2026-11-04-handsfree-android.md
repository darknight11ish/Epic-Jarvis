- **2026-11-04 — A spoken yes answers an approval card in hands-free mode, and the phone may send a text message with `SEND_SMS`:** the owner's request of 2026-11-04 settles two questions this project had already written down, and both are recorded here rather than left to be inferred from the code. **(a) A voice yes counts, in hands-free mode, for every card raised for a task he asked Jarvis to do.** This is his own security decision. It does not weaken `CLAUDE.md`'s "the app never auto-approves anything": every card is still raised by the PC, still pending until the owner's answer arrives, and the answer is still the owner's - what changes is the channel it arrives on. The authority stays on the PC, which is why the phone only ever uploads a clip: the client still never does speech-to-text, and the PC transcribes it, checks it against the owner's voice print and decides, so a `not_you` answer approves nothing. Voice does NOT begin to answer cards outside this mode, and the old rule stands elsewhere: `CardVoice` still only says where a card is. **(b) Jarvis may WRITE a text message for him, which needs `SEND_SMS` and `READ_CONTACTS`.** `docs/UI-CUTTING-EDGE-2026-10-09.md` §16.7 A3 had settled "you say send" as *hand it to my SMS app* - `Intent.ACTION_SENDTO` with an `sms_body` and **no `SEND_SMS`** - precisely because the owner presses Send in the other app. He has now asked for hands-free texting, where he does not press anything, so that route cannot do it; the later word is his, and it replaces A3 for this feature only. What the permission is bounded by, so a later reader does not have to re-derive it: the app asks for `SEND_SMS` at runtime when hands-free is switched on (never at launch), sends exactly ONE message per `phone_command`, only after the name resolved to exactly one number in the phone's own address book, never reports "sent" without Android's own `RESULT_OK`, and never becomes the default SMS app - Tier C1 of the same section, still refused. `READ_CONTACTS` is for the name only: no contact, number or message is stored, and none is sent to the PC. **(c) The phone half is one-sided on purpose:** the mouth, the ears and the SMS hand are the phone's; the decision is the PC's (`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §8, and the four routes are `phone-only` in `tools/check_parity.py` until the desktop's own half of the same contract lands).
