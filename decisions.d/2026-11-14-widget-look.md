- **2026-11-14 — A widget's look is three closed lists the owner picks, and see-through is alpha only:** The owner asked on 2026-11-14 for widgets to have their own look, choosing it over proving the existing widget on his phone and over a widget-carrying mini app. So a described widget now carries an `accent` (one of eight named colours), a `shade` (`solid`, `half`, `clear`) and a `contrast` (`normal`, `high`): three closed lists, absent meaning today's cyan on near-black, so a widget saved before this date draws as it did to within a shade - the same near-black at full opacity where it was 86% twice over, and the look's own two near-whites where the theme's were - neither visible on a dark desktop, and written down because "exactly the same" is a claim a screenshot could contradict. The model maps a colour word onto a name and is told to leave all three out unless a look was asked for; plain code checks it, and an off-list value is refused rather than defaulted. The preview's own pickers can change the look before Add, and what is saved is what the owner last looked at. The decision worth recording is the LIMIT, and it is the same one as the arrangements for a different reason: a colour and an alpha are runtime values, so they CAN be per widget, where a layout cannot - there is no fifth arrangement and no free hex, no image, no custom font and no grid. See-through means alpha over whatever is behind the card and NOTHING else, because a RemoteViews layout has no backdrop blur and neither has a webview, so the words never say "frosted" or "glass". Text is never given an alpha. Every colour on the three lists is asserted readable at WCAG AA 4.5:1 on both backgrounds in both contrasts, measured in backend/test_widgets.py rather than eyeballed, because a list of colours is only safe to offer if nothing on it can make a widget unreadable. On the PC translucency was NOT real before this change even though the window is transparent and its CSS already used rgba - the window's own shell painted an 86% fill over everything, so a 50% card composited to about 93% near-black and the shade controlled nothing - so board mode now gives that backdrop away and the card is the only see-through part of the window. On the phone no widget in this app has ever drawn with alpha (all 23 colours in widget/ are FF), so that half ships implemented and unit-checked with its first launcher look still owed. Nothing new is approved: same sources, same five buttons, the same App-lock hiding and the same stale-link gate, no new route, and the arrangement rules are untouched.
