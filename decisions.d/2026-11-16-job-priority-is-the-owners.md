- **2026-11-16 — Moving a background job's priority is the owner's act, and a model may never do it:** The owner asked (2026-11-16) to prioritise background work "through chat and otherwise". A priority change grants nothing - it reorders work that was already allowed to run, changes no capability set, starts nothing, answers no card, and never makes a job run before its own `due` time - so it belongs on the cardless-by-design list beside "the next message's lane" and the one-job schedule row, and no approval card is raised. But it is a lever over the owner's ATTENTION, so WHO may pull it is a policy question, not a UI one. The ruling: only the owner's own sentence (the fixed grammar in `jarvis_quick.py`, which answers before the model is called) and, later, the owner's own UI may set it; a MODEL may not. That is enforced by absence rather than by a check - `jarvis_jobs.set_priority()` is the only writer, `jarvis_quick.py` is its only caller, and this change adds no HTTP route, no model tool and no handler-facing writer. Why refuse a model even though the change is small: a job's label and params can come from outside the PC (the watch jobs read GitHub and their own docstring says their text is never handed to a model), so a reorder tool would be a lever that a watched page or an email could pull over what the owner sees first. If the owner later wants the model to help, the recorded shape is: LOWERING is free, RAISING raises a card - a design round, not this change. Two existing ideas stay deliberately separate: `jarvis_arbiter.py`'s `priority` is a TEXT urgency on an intents row (how loudly a finding is delivered), not this INTEGER order on a jobs row, and the two are not unified here; and "set a job's priority when it is created" is left out, because it needs `create(priority=)` and both start forms. The apps show priority in a later PR; the field is already on `GET /api/jobs`.
