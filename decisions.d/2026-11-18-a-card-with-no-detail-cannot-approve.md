- **2026-11-18 — A card that cannot say what it would change must not be approvable, and the detail is the field that says it:** the owner was shown a live `change_own_config` card about his cloud settings with no detail and a normal Approve, and the desktop HUD had the same shape with "(no detail)" as its body. The judgement calls, all taken from that measured card: (1) **The DETAIL is what the rule reads, not the notice.** `notice.summary`/`notice.full` are built from the action NAME and the risk table by `jarvis_gate.notice_for` - safe by construction on a lock screen - so they can say "change one of its settings" but never which setting or to what. Treating a readable notice as "the card is described" would have left the measured blank cheque passing. (2) **The renderer stopped deleting a detail it can put in words.** `normalisePendingRow` had removed `detail` when it matched the `summary` it derived from the same text; that was true when the card drew `item.summary`, and stopped being true when approval cards read short (c08d768a1, 2026-10-10) began drawing the PC's `notice.summary` instead. One line of text now appears twice on an opened card whose command is also its fallback summary - accepted, because losing the words the owner is agreeing to is the blank cheque. (3) **Refresh REPLACES Approve, rather than a disabled Approve.** DEF-010's lesson (a faded control captioned by a line nobody connects to it reads as a bug) and the ruling that refusing is never held: the card draws Deny on the left exactly as before, and a Refresh that re-reads the waiting queue (a read: approves nothing, never held by a stale link) where Approve would be. (3b) **The notification surface follows the card.** `ApprovalNotificationPolicy` already replaces Approve with Review in Jarvis when its words are redacted or too long; a card whose detail did not arrive is the same case (its notice words name the action, not the change), so it gets Review and Deny there and is never approved sight-unseen; the widget never offers Approve at all (it opens the app), so it needed nothing. (4) **Both apps, one copy of the words.** The phone's `net/CardDetail.kt` and the desktop HUD draw the same sentence and the same action label, and both come from `backend/jarvis_card_words.py`'s `NO_DETAIL` / `REFRESH` through `tools/gen_card_words_cases.py` into each app's contract fixture - the same one-source mechanism `TIMED_OUT` and `BUTTONS` already use; the HUD page cannot import a module, so its own constants are pinned to the module by `tests/card-words.mjs`. (5) **What did NOT change:** the backend's payload, the gate, any tier, route or decision path, the two apps' button order, and the phone's behaviour on every card that has a detail. A card that genuinely carries no detail loses its Approve on both screens - that is the decision, not a side effect.
