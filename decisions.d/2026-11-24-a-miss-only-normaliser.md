- **2026-11-24 — A near-worded request is retried through a normaliser, never rewritten:** The owner asked whether Atlas understands a sentence that is *close* to the right one, and the answer was measured, not argued: 54 plausible sentences driven through `jarvis_quick.match` reached code in 26 cases (48%). The misses were almost all the same miss — "tell me what tools you have" and "what tools are currently on" mean "what tools do you have" — and a set of whole-sentence patterns cannot see it. `jarvis_quick.flex_normalise` now strips politeness, expands contractions and maps the measured near-wordings onto their canonical sentence, and `match` retries it **only after the verbatim sentence and its meta-instruction retry both found nothing**. That order is the decision: the normaliser can add a route and can never take one away, so no sentence that already worked changes its answer, and the no-regression list in `backend/test_chat_flexibility.py` pins the guarantee. Measured after the change on the same battery: **37 of 54 (69%)**, with tool questions going from 3/10 to 10/10, approval 3/4 to 4/4 and connections 5/6 to 6/6; reminders, notes, settings and GitHub are unchanged because their misses are routing problems, not wordings, and stay their own rows (DEF-092, DEF-095, DEF-096). The alternative — rewriting the incoming text and handing the rewritten sentence on — was rejected: it would have changed what a scheduler or a notes tool receives from sentences that already work.
