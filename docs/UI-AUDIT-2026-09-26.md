# Jarvis UI audit, 2026-09-26: "spice it up without overwhelming"

The chair's report. It pulls together six team reports, kept in `docs/ui-audit-2026-09-26/` with the picture page `ui-mockups.html` (open it in a browser):
`desktop-today.md` and `phone-today.md` (how the apps look now), `research.md`
(what the best assistants do in 2025-26), `alive.md` (motion), `restraint.md`
(the rules that stop "spice" turning into clutter) and `critic.md` (a
devil's-advocate pass over all of it). Nothing in the repo was changed.

**How far to trust it.** I re-opened the claims this report leans on hardest.
They matched the repo at commit `e656093`, and they are marked **(re-checked)**
below. Everything else is taken from a team report that cited `path:line`, and
the report is named. Nobody ran the phone app, and nobody looked at the desktop
on a real Windows PC (section 6).

Paths: `desk/` means `jarvis-desktop/src/`, and `phone/` means
`jarvis-client/app/src/main/java/com/jarvis/client/`.

---

## 1. Summary (10 lines)

1. Both apps already have a clear, careful look. They do **not** need a redesign. What they need is more life in the face, and less clutter everywhere else.
2. The biggest single upgrade: **the desktop face should move with your voice and with Jarvis's real voice.** The phone does this already. The desktop fakes it (re-checked).
3. The desktop's Jarvis bar runs **seven animations at once** while an answer arrives. Cut that to one. That is calmer, and it costs less too.
4. The phone shows answers as **raw text**, so `**bold**` and `- lists` show their symbols. It needs simple formatting, like the desktop has.
5. On both apps the text sizes are **too flat and too small**. A short, fixed list of sizes, plus a few big numbers, adds more "wow" than any effect would.
6. The phone's Brain screen is **one scroll of about 33 sections**, and it is still called "Mind". Rename it and group it (you already decided the name).
7. Three real look bugs are cheap to fix. Onboarding teaches the **wrong colour for "thinking"** (it shows the approval amber). Three windows use a colour that is **never defined**. The HUD window has **its own colours**, and they fail the colour-blind and contrast rules.
8. Reduced motion (the Windows/Android "fewer animations" setting) behaves differently in each window. The desktop face **still shakes** under it (re-checked).
9. Turned down: edge glows, see-through "glass", Google's new bouncy style, AI-drawn screens, new gestures, and a new body font. Each one clashes with readability, battery, security or a choice you already made.
10. Three short questions for you are in section 5. Everything else can go ahead under the rules in section 2.

## 2. The shared direction: "Instrument Deck, alive"

This is not a new style. **Instrument Deck** is the look you approved on
2026-09-23 (`docs/UI-AUDIT-2026-09-23.md:51-59`): the app as a precise
instrument panel, with the face as its main gauge, thin panel edges instead of
shadows, status always written in words, and one moving thing at a time. This
audit keeps that and adds one idea: **the one living part should really be
alive.** The face listens, breathes and answers with the real voice. Everything
around it stays still, sharp and readable. The "spice" comes from better type,
honest motion on the face, and taking clutter away. It does not come from
glow, glass or extra animation.

**The one test every idea had to pass** (from `research.md` idea 15 and
`restraint.md` Rule 2): *anything new that moves or glows must stand for a
state, and each state gets one visual signal.* A signal already on screen,
such as the face, wins over adding a new one.

**What stays exactly as it is:**
- The three themes and their names: Reactor, Daylight and High Contrast. They are tested in both apps (`restraint.md` Rule 6).
- The animated face, all 20 designs, its colour language, and its flash limit (the cap on light-dark flicker that protects people who are sensitive to flashing).
- **Amber means "waiting on you"**, and nothing else.
- Approval cards, errors, security prompts, App lock and lock-screen notifications get **no decoration at all**: no glow, no new animation, no pictures, no jokes. The only allowed changes make them easier to read. Nothing ever makes Approve look more tempting than Deny, and nothing approves by itself.
- The contrast, colour-blind and accessibility tests (`desk/../tests/contrast.mjs`, `a11y.mjs`, `themecheck.mjs`, `distinct.mjs`, `flashgov.mjs`; on the phone `ThemeContrastTest`, `FaceBudgetTest`, `DisplayRatePolicyTest`). They must all still pass, and none of them is loosened.
- The phone's battery rules: no shadows, no live blur, one live face preview at a time, and the face's well (its dark round background) stays dark.
- The phone's own font. You chose it (`phone/ui/theme/JarvisTheme.kt:301`, re-checked).
- No more arc-reactor imagery than there is today. `restraint.md` Rule 8 notes that Marvel holds the JARVIS trademark, and the risk grows with Iron Man visuals.

**The budget per screen** (`restraint.md`): approval cards, errors, security and the desktop widget get **0** new visual ideas. Home / the Jarvis bar, Brain, and onboarding/pairing get **1** each. Settings get **0 new switches**, but tidying them is fine.

### Not for Jarvis (and why)

| Idea | Why not |
|---|---|
| **Glowing screen edge while listening** (Siri/Gemini style) | It adds a second moving thing. Listening's colour is too close to approval's for red-green colour-blind eyes (a difference of 4.5, where 2 or less looks identical; `critic.md` R9). And it is the look most likely to date first. |
| **See-through "glass" panels** (Apple's Liquid Glass) | Apple itself turned it down in 2026 because text was hard to read. Jarvis's contrast tests exist for exactly this. |
| **Mica backdrop on Brain and Settings** | It is not "one call". Both windows are built opaque (`src-tauri/src/windows.rs:418-431`, `:470-482`, no `transparent`, re-checked). Every colour would have to be re-measured on three themes, for a faint wallpaper tint. |
| **Google's "Material 3 Expressive"** (bouncy shapes, wavy progress bars) | The library is still experimental. Wavy bars are a new loop, and very 2025. The good part, "one clearly marked main action", already holds. |
| **AI-drawn screens** (the model writes the layout) | It would run code a local model just wrote, inside a window that holds your pairing key. That is a security problem, not a style choice. |
| **New gestures or card decks** (Rabbit, Humane) | They are hard to learn. Keep tap, swipe back, and the existing swipe on approval cards. |
| **A new body font for both apps** | It goes against your earlier choice of the phone's own font. The desktop's body font is already a variable one. Headings are question 3. |
| **Wallpaper colours / Windows accent colour** | They can't be measured in advance, and they would break the tested themes. |
| **A "shimmer" on status text, a typing spinner, the phone's planned streaming line** | Each is a second moving thing next to the face. |
| **An inward ring for each tool step** | Rings already mean "you tapped me" and "approval knocking". A third kind of ring weakens the knock. |
| **Cards that grow into their page** (container transforms) | It would keep two screens alive at once, including Home with the face. That is a battery cost for a flourish. |
| **Dot-matrix style, extra GPU glow, gyro parallax, text orbiting the face, particles** | Borrowed styles, more battery, or harder to read. |
| **Pictures in empty states, icons on every row, any new appearance setting** | Decoration. Empty lists get one helpful next step in words instead. The phone already has about 15 look settings. |

## 4. Things to REMOVE or simplify

Taking away is the biggest win in this audit. Worst first:

1. **The phone's one-scroll Brain** (about 33 sections of status, settings and memory mixed together). Group it now (item 9), and later move settings out (item 14).
2. **Five of the seven loops on the Jarvis bar** while an answer streams, and the ring spin when idle (item 2).
3. **The HUD's private colour set**: about 114 hard-coded colours, two groups sharing one colour, and labels too faint to read (item 11).
4. **About 20 loose desktop font sizes**, replaced by 4-5 sizes (item 8).
5. **Four different reduced-motion behaviours**, replaced by one (item 3).
6. **"Core / Ollama / LiteLLM" on every answer**, replaced by nothing when fine and a plain sentence when not (item 18).
7. **The 10-row shortcut table** on a bar meant to feel light (item 19).
8. **Developer readouts** in the Faces window, and machine text such as hashes and file paths where a plain sentence would do (`desktop-today.md` cross-cutting point 6; the approval card's own wording is not touched here).
9. **Three on/off switch styles, three "Local" badge styles and three different Jarvis logos** on the desktop (`desktop-today.md` point 5). Pick one of each. This needs a separate look before anything is merged.
10. **The widget's solid green Approve.** In the bar, Approve is a tinted fill (`desk/style.css:1629-1633`). In the widget it is solid bright green (`desk/widget.css:724-727`, re-checked), which makes it more eye-catching than Deny. See question 2.

## 5. Questions for you

**1. The HUD window** (the three-column "instrument" view) has its own colours. Some of them are too faint to read, and some can't be told apart by colour-blind eyes.
- **Give it the app's normal colours**, so it follows your theme too (recommended)
- **Keep it always dark**, but fix its colours so they pass the same tests

**2. The Approve button on the desktop widget** is solid bright green. In the Jarvis bar it is a softer tinted button, so the two look different, and the widget's version pulls the eye toward Approve.
- **Make the widget match the bar** (recommended)
- **Leave it as it is**

**3. Headings on the phone.** The desktop uses a squared-off "techy" font (Chakra Petch) for titles. The phone uses its own font everywhere, which you chose on 2026-09-23.
- **Keep the phone's font everywhere** (recommended: it was your choice, and bigger sizes give most of the gain)
- **Use Chakra Petch for phone titles only**

(A smaller one you don't need to answer: `alive.md` suggested slowing the
face's approval "knock" after a minute of waiting. We recommend leaving it
alone, because approvals never get quieter. Say so if you want it.)

## 6. What nobody could check

- **How the phone app looks.** No Android build is possible in this container, and the repo has no screenshots of the phone's screens. Everything about the phone comes from reading the code.
- **The desktop on a real Windows PC.** The screenshots were made in a copy of the app with a stand-in font (DejaVu Sans instead of Segoe UI) and recorded sample data.
- Whether the HUD's blur effect shows through on a real PC (Tauri has open bug reports saying it may not).
- Whether the desktop's "Local" badge really stays lit while offline. The screenshot suggests it does, but the code at `desk/main.js:941-952` switches it to "Offline" when Core is down. It is not settled.
- Whether the HUD's "CLOUD" lanes and "free-tier budget" rows are still reported by the real HUD server, or are leftover design.
- Whether an approval card that is fading out of a phone list can still be tapped during the fade (item 6 guards against it anyway).
- The exact delay between the phone face and Jarvis's voice (item 21 measures it).
- Whether the server says "speaking" while the desktop bar plays a clip (item 1 assumes it does).
- Which library versions the Live Updates chip and generated widget previews need (item 22).
- Whether an in-app vibration on a new approval would double up with the notification's vibration. That is why none is proposed.
- Whether the chat stream says which timer was just set. That would be needed for any timer card under an answer, so none is proposed now.
- **Your earlier font answer is recorded only in a code comment** (`phone/ui/theme/JarvisTheme.kt:301`). It is not in the docs, and the TODO above it (`:223-227`) still says the opposite. Worth writing down properly.
- Whether another agent is already renaming "Mind" to "Brain" on another branch.
