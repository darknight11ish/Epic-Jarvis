1 of 3 — the tray icon

Jarvis lives in your tray

Look in the row of small icons at the bottom-right of your screen, next to the clock. That dot is Jarvis — it changes colour and shape depending on what Jarvis is doing right now.

Resting — nothing to do right now
Thinking, or in the middle of answering you
Waiting on you to approve or deny something

Click it any time to open Jarvis's other windows, or to quit.

2 of 3 — approvals

Jarvis asks before anything risky

If Jarvis wants to do something it isn't sure is safe — like sending a message, or changing a file — it stops and shows you a card first. The card waits for you in the Jarvis bar, on the widget, or on your phone's Home screen (Alt+Space opens the Jarvis bar), with two buttons: Approve and Deny. Nothing like that happens without you saying yes.

You can always say no, and nothing bad happens if you do. There is no setting anywhere that makes Jarvis stop asking and start approving things on its own.

A few things Jarvis already answers without waiting on the AI model:

  • “Set a timer for 10 minutes.”
  • “What did I miss?”
  • “Remind me to call Mom at 6pm.”

3 of 3 — memory

Jarvis learns from what you tell it

Jarvis saves facts about you and your projects from your own words — what you type or say to it. Never from emails, web pages, documents or files.

Every fact it saves is listed, with a Forget button, in the Brain window, under Memory. Health, money, passwords and other people's private details are different: those wait for your yes first. You can turn automatic learning off there too.
