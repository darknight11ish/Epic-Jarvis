1 of 5 — the tray icon

Jarvis lives in your tray

Look in the row of small icons at the bottom-right of your screen, next to the clock. That dot is Jarvis — it changes colour and shape depending on what Jarvis is doing right now. Not there? Look under the ^ arrow by the clock.

Resting — nothing to do right now
Thinking, or in the middle of answering you
Waiting on you to approve or deny something

Click it any time to open Jarvis's other windows, or to quit.

2 of 5 — approvals

Jarvis asks before anything risky

If Jarvis wants to do something it isn't sure is safe — like sending a message, or changing a file — it stops and shows you a card first. The card waits for you in the Jarvis bar, on the widget, or on your phone's Home screen (Alt+Space opens the Jarvis bar), with two buttons: Approve and Deny. Nothing like that happens without you saying yes.

The card is one action, and only that one: approving it does not allow that kind of thing in future, and there is no “approve everything” button anywhere. You can always say no, and nothing bad happens if you do. Settings, “What asks first” lists what asks.

A few things Jarvis already answers without waiting on the AI model:

  • “Set a timer for 10 minutes.”
  • “What did I miss?”
  • “Remind me to call Mom at 6pm.”

3 of 5 — memory

Jarvis learns from what you tell it

Jarvis saves facts about you and your projects from your own words — what you type or say to it. Never from emails, web pages, documents or files.

Every fact it saves is listed, with a Forget button, in the Brain window, under Memory. Health, money, passwords and other people's private details are different: those wait for your yes first. You can turn automatic learning off there too.

4 of 5 — talking to it

Say it, or type it

Press Alt+Space and the Jarvis bar comes up over whatever you are doing. Type your question and press Enter, or hold the microphone button and speak. Esc puts the bar away again.

A few things work even when the AI model is busy or asleep, so they answer straight away — timers, reminders, your lists, and “what did I miss?”. The bar shows those on its own when it is empty; click one to put it in the box.

Jarvis also lives in the tray, next to the clock — right-click that icon for the same windows, and for Quit.

5 of 5 — the settings

Everything else is in Settings

Right-click the tray icon and choose Settings, or ask Jarvis to open a section of it. The page has a jump list down the left, and every card is a plain sentence about what it does. Nothing there is changed for you: you make each change yourself.

Two pages are worth knowing on day one. “What asks first” lists every action and whether it waits for your yes. “What Jarvis can reach” lists every way out of this PC that is switched on right now — start there if you ever wonder what it can touch.

Jarvis runs on this PC: the model, your memory and your chat history are all on this machine, and anything private stays here.
